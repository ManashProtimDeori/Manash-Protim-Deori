# Canonical Strategy Lab v5 — Iterations 21–30

This round integrates the WIN insight-creation and narrative guidance into the existing Canonical Strategy Lab while preserving the evidence, model and executive-quality controls from the prior versions.

| Iteration | Test | Gap identified | Improvement integrated |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 21 | Narrative architecture | The deck moved from company strategy to analysis without an explicit candidate bridge. | Rebuilt the opening around a dedicated introduction and connected the strategic system to the candidate contribution narrative. |
| 22 | Three-ideas discipline | The three strategic bets existed, but their role as the memorable core of the story was not explicit enough. | Enterprise assurance, private-cloud migration and neutral AI infrastructure are now presented as the three-ideas framework with trigger, motion, KPI and falsifier logic. |
| 23 | Research-to-action loop | Research inputs were distributed across the deck rather than expressed as one campaign operating loop. | Added a repeatable loop across campaign analysis, search intent, social/brand listening, customer and partner feedback, experiments, measurement and iteration. |
| 24 | Requirement-to-evidence mapping | Candidate fit could be inferred but was not mapped to Canonical’s stated marketing expectations. | Added explicit mapping from campaign ownership, analytics, storytelling, cross-functional work and hands-on execution to candidate evidence. |
| 25 | Track-record integrity | A stronger candidate narrative could tempt unsupported performance claims. | Prior experience is stated at responsibility level only; confidential or unavailable outcomes are not invented. |
| 26 | Technical-transfer credibility | Engineering training and analytical work needed to be translated without overstating direct enterprise-software marketing tenure. | Technical and quantitative fluency are positioned as transferable strengths while the direct enterprise-tech marketing gap is explicitly acknowledged. |
| 27 | Candidate differentiation | The strategy showed what Canonical could do, but not clearly enough what the candidate would personally operationalize. | Added two dedicated “How I can help Canonical” slides: operating-system contribution and evidence-of-fit/transferability. |
| 28 | Export completeness | The live deck could be exported as PPTX but not as a presentation-ready PDF. | Added direct multi-page PDF generation from the rendered slide sequence alongside editable PPTX export. |
| 29 | Brand and opening craft | The first slide still carried internal model-governance language that weakened the introduction. | Removed the model-governance box, added a Canonical-inspired identity lockup, candidate name and a clean strategic positioning statement. |
| 30 | Anti-generic stress test | Candidate and strategy slides were re-read for statements that could fit any B2B company. | Tightened the narrative around Canonical-specific triggers: open-source adoption, paid assurance, migration economics, partner leverage, secure AI, recurring attach and community trust. |

## v5 narrative rules

1. The opening establishes the company, the candidate and the strategic thesis immediately.
2. The strategy is organized around three memorable ideas rather than a long list of tactics.
3. Each idea connects research signal → operating motion → KPI → falsifier.
4. Candidate evidence is mapped to role requirements without invented outcomes.
5. Direct enterprise-technology marketing experience is not overstated.
6. High-stakes stakeholder work is described without exposing confidential client details.
7. The candidate narrative remains subordinate to the quality of the Canonical strategy itself.
8. PPTX and PDF exports preserve the live deck’s ordering and presentation logic.
9. Reported facts, model outputs and candidate-provided background remain distinct evidence classes.
10. Any claim that cannot survive source, arithmetic or experience-level scrutiny is removed rather than embellished.
