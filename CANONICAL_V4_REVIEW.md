# Canonical Strategy Lab v4 — Ten additional hardening iterations

These ten iterations are layered on top of the original 20 stakeholder lenses and the first 10 CMO refinements. They focus on evidence quality, legibility, decision language and fundamental marketing logic.

| Iteration | Question | Gap found | Change integrated |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 11 | Can every sentence be read comfortably without relying on theme state or low-opacity text? | The interface still used many 28–56% white text utilities and muted neutral text. | Contrast, minimum font sizes and source-footer legibility were increased across the Canonical surface. |
| 12 | Does the leadership box sound like an instruction rather than an insight? | Several boxes used direct imperatives such as “use”, “sell”, “build” and “set”. | The box was renamed “Leadership insight” and every slide insight was rewritten as professional passive-voice analysis. |
| 13 | Can a reported number survive independent source triangulation? | The deck mixed verified facts and model outputs without a single evidence appendix. | A verified-evidence registry was added, and externally reported facts are retained only where reliable references are consistent. |
| 14 | Can a reader tell reported facts from modeled outputs instantly? | Scenario outputs could visually resemble audited results. | Appendix rules now separate reported facts, assumptions, calculations and directional indices explicitly. |
| 15 | Are PDF page numbers ever guessed? | Page-level references were not systematically governed. | PDF page numbers are shown only when verified; when a registry index cannot expose a pinpoint page, the limitation is stated instead of inventing one. |
| 16 | Does the strategy contain ideas that remain useful after the current news cycle changes? | Current-product developments were strong, but foundational principles were distributed across slides. | A dedicated “fundamental marketing laws” slide was added around option pools, asymmetric risk, evidence accumulation, control optionality, default formation and cognitive compression. |
| 17 | Is competitor scale being confused with competitor strategic quality? | Large revenue figures can psychologically dominate comparison even when the business models differ. | Scale figures are now explicitly framed as context for distribution power, not as like-for-like company performance rankings. |
| 18 | Is “marketing rescue” being used where the real issue is product or operations? | Some responses risked implying that marketing can repair structural economics. | A stricter principle was added: marketing diagnoses, de-risks and reallocates before amplifying when retention, migration, services or product friction deteriorates. |
| 19 | Can every strategic claim be invalidated? | Falsifiers existed at variable level but not as a universal evidence norm. | The appendix and governance language now make falsifiability a standing requirement for strategy publication. |
| 20 | Would a senior executive know which numbers should be trusted at a glance? | Source transparency was fragmented across slide footers. | A dedicated appendix slide now exposes claim, retained value, primary source, cross-check, link, PDF page policy and verification note. |

## v4 principles

1. Legibility is treated as a decision-quality requirement, not a cosmetic preference.
2. Leadership boxes contain insights, not commands.
3. Reported facts must be triangulated; model outputs must be labeled as model outputs.
4. No PDF page number is invented.
5. No competitor scale metric is presented as a like-for-like valuation of strategic strength.
6. Brand indices remain directional diagnostics and are not treated as audited brand equity.
7. Marketing-influenced ARR remains non-additive to company revenue.
8. Fundamental insights must describe mechanisms, not slogans.
9. Marketing is not used as a rhetorical substitute for product, delivery or customer-success fixes.
10. Every important strategic claim should carry either evidence, a model path or a falsifier.
